Best of the best, Tunkillia’s golden finale for Barton Gold

by Ethan Benedicto
October 8, 2026
in Gold, Mining Companies, News
Reading Time: 4 mins read
A A

Image: Phawat/stock.adobe.com

Barton Gold has completed the assay phase of its major Tunkillia resource-upgrade campaign, with final results delivering broad, high-grade intersections inside the South Australian project’s proposed starter pits.

The latest batch included 53m at 2.51 grams per tonne (g/t) gold from 60m in hole TKB0745, including 2m at 18.25g/t from 72m and 1m at 26.3g/t from 92m.

Hole TKB0742 returned 21m at 4.13g/t from 61m, including 2m at 29.9g/t from 79m, while TKB0724 intersected 26m at 2.79g/t from 126m, including 1m at 53.5g/t.

Other results included 14m at 3.80g/t from 67m, including 2m at 11g/t, and 17m at 2.26g/t from 116m, including 1m at 20.7g/t.

The assays complete Barton’s 38,760m phase 2 campaign across 311 holes, and together, the phase 1 and phase 2 programs delivered 57,653m of reverse-circulation and diamond drilling across 520 holes.

Barton managing director Alexander Scanlon said the results reinforced both earlier drilling and the interpretation of additional shallow mineralisation.

“We are pleased with the results of Tunkillia’s ‘phase 2’ infill assays, which support the highest-grade results of ‘phase 1’ drilling and the potential for new, shallower zones of high-grade mineralisation within the existing ‘starter pits’,” Scanlon said.

The strongest final assays came from the planned S1 and S2 pits within the main Area 223 open pit, where the current model has an average grade of 1.05g/t gold, the same pits which are intended to provide Tunkillia’s higher-value early production.

Mid-September Phase 2 results had supported a shallow mineralised zone above material already included in the S1 pit model. The new batch completes the dataset needed to test that interpretation through updated geological modelling.

Barton said the latest drilling indicated mineralisation in the starter-pit area may be more vertically controlled than previously modelled, potentially improving the continuity of primary lodes. Mineralised oxidised mafic dyke material was also identified within 18m of surface.

At Area 51, phase 2 drilling confirmed a thick, higher-grade shoot extending approximately 250m north and 200m south across separate sections. The mineralisation remains open to the south and west, and drilling at Area 223 North also identified mineralisation at or beyond the limits used in the previous pit profile.

The reported intersections are downhole lengths, and their effect on Tunkillia’s resource, mine plan and economics has not yet been determined.

Tunkillia currently hosts 1.6 million ounces of gold and 3.1 million ounces of silver, and Barton has begun remodelling the deposit, with an updated gold mineral resource expected first and an updated silver resource planned for the first quarter of 2027.

“We are now remodelling all Tunkillia mineralisation with the benefit of nearly 58,000m additional drilling since last year,” Scanlon said.

“A JORC resources update for gold is pending, and a JORC update for silver and a pre-feasibility study are planned for the first quarter of the 2027 calendar year.”

The updates will feed into the pre-feasibility study being led by GR Engineering Services.

Barton’s May 2025 optimised scoping study outlined a potential operation producing about 120,000 ounces of gold and 250,000 ounces of silver annually, with a $1.4 billion unlevered pre-tax net present value, a 73.2 per cent internal rate of return and a payback period of about 0.8 years under the study assumptions.

Those projections pre-date the latest drilling and remain subject to the PFS and further technical work.

The development program is also moving through approvals. The South Australian Government recently approved Tunkillia’s environmental scoping report, establishing the terms of reference for environmental studies, consultation and other work required for the mining lease process.

Barton is progressing flora, fauna, water and cultural heritage programs alongside tailings, infrastructure, accommodation and renewable-energy studies.

The company intends to lodge a mining lease application after the PFS, which remains targeted for the first quarter of 2027.
